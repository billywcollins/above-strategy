The Market

What's true about the US market right now.

"Innovation feels like a luxury. But the gap between physical and digital product design has never been wider — or more costly."

What we know to be true

Tight budgets driven by economic uncertainty have made "innovation" feel out of touch with the reality of running a profitable business. Leaders are risk-averse, and large discretionary design engagements are harder to justify than they were five years ago.

Companies want to move on AI — but they don't want to outsource that capability entirely. They want direction and activation, not dependency.

The decline of IDEO, Fjord, and Gravity Tank as independent forces isn't just a business story. It's a market signal: the era of the immersive, expensive, agency-led transformation engagement is over. Clients want outcomes, not process theater.

What's shifting in our favor

Companies that built in-house design and innovation teams over the last decade are quietly discovering that internal teams struggle with the same problem they always did: proximity breeds timidity. They have capability but lack the external perspective and creative courage to place bold bets.

Connected products are creating design complexity that most agencies are genuinely unequipped to handle. Experiences that span physical hardware, digital interfaces, and real-world context require a kind of integrated thinking that almost no one does well.

Boutique firms with genuine craft depth are winning work that large consultancies used to own — but almost none of them can bridge physical and digital as a single integrated problem. That gap is where Above lives.

The Opportunity

The specific gap Above is built to fill.

The question isn't whether there's a market for design and innovation services in the US. There is — and it's growing. The question is what kind of firm wins in this specific moment, with these specific conditions.

What the market has

Agencies that are excellent at digital product design, or excellent at industrial design. Firms that can make beautiful screens, or beautiful objects. Development shops that can build what someone else imagined.

Consultancies that will tell clients what they want to hear, wrap it in a framework, and call it a strategy.

vs

What the market needs

A partner who operates with equal fluency across physical and digital — and treats them as one problem, not two parallel workstreams.

A firm with the creative courage and cultural honesty to tell a client when their brief is wrong, when the concept has a flaw, and when the market won't receive what they think it will.

Observation 01

The integration gap is real and widening

Most companies building connected products today are managing two separate creative relationships — one for the hardware, one for the digital experience. The seam between them is where products fail.

Observation 02

Long-term creative partners are scarce

The project-based agency model is exhausting for clients. The EarLabs model — a decade-long creative and commercial partnership that helped build a company — is rare and genuinely valued when clients find it.

Observation 03

Scandinavian honesty is a differentiator

US agencies optimize for winning the room. Scandinavian design culture optimizes for getting it right. In a market full of yes, a credible no — delivered with rigor and care — is worth a great deal.

Where We Play

Three verticals. Different entry points. One thesis.

Above should not pursue all markets simultaneously. The following verticals are prioritized by a combination of portfolio fit, network warmth, and market readiness — not just size of prize. Early wins that are the right wins matter more than large engagements that stretch our credibility.

01 — Healthcare & Health Tech

Above's strongest US foothold. The Siemens relationship in Boston was organically built from Sweden — proof that our work speaks before we do. The portfolio here is genuinely compelling: INTO light therapy, EarLabs ear protection, PACT personal massage device, Avanto Care eldercare platform.

The US healthcare market is undergoing fundamental transformation — toward home-based care, connected diagnostics, and patient-facing devices that need to be both clinically rigorous and humanly desirable. That intersection is precisely where Above operates.

Entry point: Leverage existing relationships (Siemens Boston, regional medical practice) as reference clients and proof of category credibility. Target innovation and product leadership at mid-to-large health systems and medical device companies.

Priority 1 Network: Warm Portfolio: Strong
02 — Consumer & Lifestyle Products

Above's deepest and most diverse case portfolio. The EarLabs, Bang & Olufsen, and Beosound cases demonstrate an ability to make products that become iconic in their categories — not just beautiful, but resolved. The live flagship connected hardware opportunity currently in play makes this vertical immediately tangible.

US consumer brands in outdoor, wellness, audio, and sports categories are actively looking for the kind of long-term creative partnership Above offers naturally. They move faster than enterprise clients, take more creative risk, and reward genuine craft.

Entry point: Convert the current live opportunity into a reference case. Use the Frog alumni BD network to open doors at consumer product innovation leads. Build a narrative around the EarLabs model — creative partner, not vendor.

Priority 2 Live Opportunity Portfolio: Deepest
03 — Hard Tech & Industrial Manufacturers

The most structurally compelling vertical for Above's physical-digital capability set — but the hardest to enter without a warm door. Companies like Milwaukee Tool, Bosch, and John Deere are deeply invested in making their physical products smarter: connected jobsite platforms, app-controlled hardware, digital interfaces on industrial equipment.

Above is one of very few firms that can credibly operate across industrial design and digital product simultaneously without treating them as separate workstreams. The ABB A400 EV charger case is a direct proof point for this audience. The challenge is access — these companies have significant in-house design capability and require relationship-based entry.

Entry point: Research where Above's European hard tech relationships have US counterparts or subsidiaries. Identify industry events (trade shows, design conferences) where innovation and product leadership congregates. Build a targeted outreach strategy for the right titles at the right companies.

Priority 3 Network: Cold Portfolio: Targeted
How We Win

A positioning narrative built to open doors.

Above's current description — "a Scandinavian innovation and design agency with a unique blend of digital and physical design capabilities" — is accurate but inside-out. It describes what we are. It doesn't answer the question a prospect is actually asking: what problem do you solve for me, and why can't anyone else solve it the same way?

The Opener — Lead with the problem
"We work with companies who are trying to make a physical product smarter, or a digital experience more tangible — and are finding that most agencies can do one or the other, but not both."

This framing is industry-agnostic, invites self-identification, and turns the microphone back to the prospect. The right client recognizes their own problem immediately.

The Differentiator — When they lean in
"What makes us different isn't just the capability. It's the working model. We're Scandinavian — which means we're going to tell you what we actually think. If the brief is wrong, we'll say so. If the concept has a flaw, we'll find it before the market does. That kind of honesty is rarer than it should be."

This reframes "Scandinavian" from a geographic descriptor into a value proposition. It's not where we're from — it's how we work, and what clients get from working with us that they won't get elsewhere.

What this positioning does

Function 01

Excludes the wrong clients

Companies looking for visual polish, trend-chasing, or yes-men will self-select out. That's a feature, not a bug. The clients who stay are the ones who will value what Above actually does.

Function 02

Earns credibility before the portfolio

Leading with the problem rather than credentials signals that we understand the client's world. It creates the conditions for a real conversation rather than a credentials review.

Function 03

Creates space for long relationships

Positioning around honesty and partnership naturally invites the EarLabs model — where the best work happens not in a single project, but over time, with trust built incrementally.

Next Steps

What we need to decide and do together.

This document is a starting point, not a finished strategy. The questions below represent the decisions that will shape the first 90 days of Above's US operation. Some can be answered in Stockholm. Others will only be answered in the market.

01

Align on vertical sequencing and the first 90-day focus

Healthcare and consumer products are the warmest near-term opportunities. Hard tech is the most structurally compelling long-term play. We need to agree on how to resource against each, and what a first credible US win looks like — and by when.

02

Build the US target company list together

I have hypotheses on targets across all three verticals. Above's European leadership has relationship context I don't yet have. The strongest list will combine both. This is a working session item — not a deliverable I can produce alone.

03

Define the US-Sweden working model explicitly

Who owns the client relationship at what stage? How does a US-based business lead work with a Sweden-based delivery team across time zones? What does the handoff from commercial to creative look like? Leaving this undefined creates friction that compounds quickly.

04

Pressure-test the positioning narrative with Above leadership

The opener and differentiator outlined here are hypotheses based on US market reading. Above's creative and commercial leadership will know immediately whether they ring true to the actual working culture and output. Refine together before taking to market.

05

Identify the European relationships with US counterparts

Several of Above's existing European clients — particularly in hard tech and healthcare — likely have US subsidiaries or US-based innovation teams. A warm introduction from an existing Above client relationship is the highest-quality door we can open in the US market. We should map these systematically.
